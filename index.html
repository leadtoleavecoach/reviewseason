Sound familiar?

Review season is hard on everyone

  • You sit down to write reviews and can only remember the last few weeks, so one bad month outweighs eleven good ones.
  • Your ratings come down to gut feel, and your best people can tell.
  • You've asked someone to "show more ownership" and watched them walk out more confused than when they came in.
  • You've written "be more of a team player" and hoped they'd figure out the rest.
  • Someone walks out of their review blindsided, and starts updating their résumé that night.

When a review comes from memory and gut feel, your best people start to wonder if anyone sees their work. That's when they start looking for another job, somewhere their work gets noticed.

What this session fixes

Turn reviews into the reason your people stay

1

See the whole year

Most reviews cover the last 30 days, because that's all anyone remembers. Learn how to keep a record so one bad month never erases eleven good ones, and your people know you saw all of it.

2

Rate what they did

Take the gut feel out of ratings. Measure people against clear competencies, so nobody walks in blindsided and every rating holds up when someone pushes back.

3

Run a conversation that lands

Lower the tension, show them the impact they made, and end with a plan they believe in. Done well, the review becomes a reason they stay.
